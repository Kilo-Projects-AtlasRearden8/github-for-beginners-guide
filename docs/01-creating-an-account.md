# Creating a GitHub Account

Everything on GitHub starts with an account. It is free to create one, and you can use it for public projects forever without paying.

## Step 1: Go to the sign-up page

1. Open your web browser and go to [github.com](https://github.com).
2. Click **Sign up** in the top right corner.

## Step 2: Choose your account settings

GitHub will ask you for:

- **Email address.** Use an email you check regularly. GitHub uses it to confirm your account and to send notifications.
- **Password.** Make it strong and unique. Do not reuse a password from another site.
- **Username.** This is your public name on GitHub. It appears in your profile URL, like `github.com/your-username`. You can change it later, but it is easier to pick something you like now.
- **Verify you are human.** GitHub may show a puzzle or ask you to select images. Follow the prompts.

## Step 3: Confirm your email

GitHub sends a confirmation email to the address you provided.

1. Open your email app.
2. Find the message from GitHub (check your spam folder if you do not see it).
3. Click the **Verify email address** button.

Your account is not fully active until you confirm your email.

## Step 4: Choose a plan (free is fine)

GitHub will ask you to pick a plan. Choose the **Free** plan. You can create unlimited public repositories and unlimited private repositories on the free plan. You can upgrade later if you ever need to.

## Step 5: Set up your profile (optional but helpful)

1. Click your profile picture in the top right, then **Settings**.
2. Under **Profile**, add:
   - A display name (for example, "Dylan Asdale")
   - A short bio (for example, "Nurse, AI author, founder of Asdale Dynamics, LLC")
   - A location (optional)
   - A profile picture (optional)

A filled-out profile helps people recognize you when you comment on issues or open pull requests.

## Important reminders

- **Never share your password** with anyone, including people claiming to be from GitHub support. GitHub will never ask for your password by email.
- **Do not put secrets in your repositories.** API keys, passwords, and personal data should never be committed to GitHub, even in private repositories.
- **Two-factor authentication (2FA).** After your account is created, go to Settings > Password and authentication and turn on 2FA. It adds a second step when you log in, keeping your account safer.

## What's next

With your account ready, you can create your first repository.
